## 1. THE HEADLINE FINDING — RCW 62A.2-719(3) (Washington's non-uniform UCC provision)

Washington added consumer protections to the uniform UCC text that most states
do not have. [RCW 62A.2-719(3)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=62A.2-719)
provides:

> "Limitation of remedy to repair or replacement of defective parts or
> non-conforming goods is **invalid in sales of goods primarily for personal,
> family or household use** unless the manufacturer or seller maintains or
> provides **within this state** facilities adequate to provide reasonable and
> expeditious performance of repair or replacement obligations."

**Application:** Masterbuilt's warranty limits the remedy to replacement parts.
Masterbuilt is headquartered in Columbus, GA and (on information and belief)
maintains no repair facilities in Washington — it ships parts by mail and tells
consumers to install them themselves. If Masterbuilt has no adequate in-state
repair facilities, its repair/replace-only remedy limitation is **statutorily
invalid**, and the full range of UCC buyer's remedies (refund-measure damages)
opens up. This argument should be added to the case file — it does not
currently appear in the Section 10 argument list.

## 2. FAILURE OF ESSENTIAL PURPOSE — RCW 62A.2-719(2)

Even where a repair/replace limitation is otherwise valid,
[RCW 62A.2-719(2)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=62A.2-719)
provides that "[w]here circumstances cause an exclusive or limited remedy to
**fail of its essential purpose**, remedy may be had as provided in this Title."

The nationally applied standard: the limited remedy fails of its essential
purpose when the seller is "unable to put the goods in warranted condition"
within a reasonable time — the buyer is deprived of the substantial value of
the bargain ([Cornell LII, UCC § 2-719](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-719);
see also [Masuda Funai analysis](https://www.masudafunai.com/articles/are-remedies-limitations-enforceable-when-a-limited-remedy-fails-of-its-essential-purpose)).

**Application:** This is the legal name for argument #2 in the case file
("Masterbuilt's own parts failed"). Three rounds of manufacturer-supplied
parts (Orders #803100, #946103) never restored the grill to working condition
— Failure #3 (Easter 2025) occurred **with all replacement parts installed**.
The remedy failed of its essential purpose as a matter of documented fact, which
defeats the "warranty was fulfilled" defense (Heather V.'s June 22, 2026
closure letter).

## 3. IMPLIED WARRANTY OF MERCHANTABILITY — RCW 62A.2-314

- Goods must be "fit for the ordinary purposes for which such goods are used."
  A $989 grill that suffered four distinct failures (controller, sensors, fans,
  structural ash-pan housing) within three years of light residential use is a
  textbook fitness failure.
- Statute of limitations: **RCW 62A.2-725** — four years from tender of
  delivery. Purchase July 4, 2023 → **deadline approximately July 4, 2027.**
  All claims are currently timely, but this is the outer clock.
- Disclaimers of merchantability must mention "merchantability" and be
  conspicuous ([RCW 62A.2-316](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?Cite=62A.2-316)).
  Home Depot's receipt/return policy is not a conspicuous UCC disclaimer.

### ⚠️ The privity split — this is the most important strategic nuance found

Washington case law treats the implied warranty claim **differently for each
defendant**:

**Against HOME DEPOT (strong):** Austin bought directly from Home Depot Store
#4720. Direct contractual privity exists. Home Depot is a "merchant with
respect to goods of that kind," so the RCW 62A.2-314 implied warranty attached
to the sale automatically. The case-file defenses HD raised (return policy,
"work with the manufacturer," no protection plan) do not answer a UCC warranty
claim by a buyer in privity.

**Against MASTERBUILT (weaker on implied warranty — use express warranty and
Magnuson-Moss instead):**
- *Baughn v. Honda Motor Co.*, 107 Wn.2d 127 (1986): Washington follows the
  traditional rule that a plaintiff **may not bring a UCC implied warranty
  action without contractual privity** — and Austin's contract was with Home
  Depot, not Masterbuilt. Privity is relaxed where the manufacturer makes
  **express representations** (advertising, written warranty) to the plaintiff.
- *Touchet Valley Grain Growers v. Opp & Seibold*, 119 Wn.2d 334 (1992):
  third-party-beneficiary exception to the privity rule.
- *Tex Enterprises, Inc. v. Brockway Standard, Inc.*, 149 Wn.2d 204 (2003)
  ([full opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wa-supreme-court/1436658.html)):
  confirmed Baughn/Touchet framework — implied warranties do not arise from a
  remote manufacturer's express representations absent privity or reliance.

**Strategic consequence:** Against Masterbuilt, lead with (1) breach of its
**express written warranty** (remedy failed of essential purpose, § 2 above),
(2) **Magnuson-Moss** (§ 4 below), and (3) CPA. Reserve the pure implied
warranty theory primarily for Home Depot, where privity is airtight. The
case-file Section 10 currently ranks "implied warranty" as argument #1 against
both defendants — it should be #1 against Home Depot only.

## 4. MAGNUSON-MOSS WARRANTY ACT — 15 U.S.C. § 2301 et seq.

[15 U.S.C. § 2308](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2308):

- **(a)** A supplier who gives a written warranty **may not disclaim or modify
  any implied warranty**. Masterbuilt gave a 1-year written warranty, so it
  cannot disclaim implied warranties outright.
- **(b)** Implied warranties may be limited *in duration* to the written
  warranty's duration **only if** the limitation is (i) conscionable and
  (ii) "set forth in clear and unmistakable language and **prominently
  displayed on the face of the warranty**."
- **(c)** A limitation violating the section is "**ineffective for purposes of
  this chapter and State law**."

**Action item:** Pull the actual Masterbuilt 1050 warranty document and check
whether a duration limitation on implied warranties appears prominently on its
face. If not, the implied warranty was never validly shortened to one year,
and Masterbuilt's entire "your warranty expired in 2024" position collapses —
even before reaching the RCW 62A.2-719(3) argument. Also note § 2310(d):
a prevailing consumer is entitled to costs and reasonable attorney fees
(fees are largely moot pro se, but costs are not).

## 5. WASHINGTON CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT — RCW 19.86

**Elements** — *Hangman Ridge Training Stables, Inc. v. Safeco Title Ins. Co.*,
105 Wn.2d 778 (1986)
([opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1986/51213-2-1.html)):
(1) unfair or deceptive act or practice, (2) in trade or commerce, (3) public
interest impact, (4) injury to plaintiff's business or property, (5) causation.

**Public interest is now codified** —
[RCW 19.86.093](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.86.093): the
element is satisfied if the act "(a) [i]njured other persons; (b) had the
capacity to injure other persons; or (c) has the capacity to injure other
persons." The widespread, documented pattern of identical Gravity Series
controller/fan/sensor failures (§ 8 below) satisfies this directly.

**Key supporting case — *Testo v. Russ Dunmire Oldsmobile, Inc.*, 16 Wn. App.
39 (1976)** ([opinion](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1317874/testo-v-russ-dunmire-oldsmobile/)):
a **Division Two (Tacoma) consumer-goods case**. The court held the seller's
attempted warranty disclaimers ineffective, found breach of the implied
warranty of merchantability, held the conduct violated the CPA, and awarded
**rescission — return of the full purchase price — plus attorney fees**. This
is the closest factual template found: defective consumer product, ineffective
disclaimer, refund as remedy, in the same appellate division that covers Pierce
County.

**Remedies** — [RCW 19.86.090](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.86.090):
actual damages + costs + fees, and **discretionary trebling** capped at
$25,000. Realistic frame: $989.39 trebled ≈ $2,968 — well within the small
claims $10,000 cap, and the trebling exposure is what makes a ~$500–$989
settlement economically rational for defendants (case-file argument #6 —
confirmed sound).

**Candid caveat:** trebling is discretionary and a small claims judge may award
only actual damages. Also, deceptive-act framing matters: the strongest CPA
hooks are (a) continuing to sell a product line with known systemic defects,
(b) Masterbuilt's "warranty fulfilled" representation when the remedy never
cured the defect, and (c) the incorrect purchase date asserted to the BBB.

## 6. SMALL CLAIMS MECHANICS — RCW 12.40 (Pierce County)

- Jurisdictional limit **$10,000** for natural persons
  ([Ch. 12.40 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/Rcw/default.aspx?cite=12.40&full=true)).
- **RCW 12.40.080:** no attorneys may appear **without the judge's consent** —
  this neutralizes Home Depot's "legal team prepared" posture; a corporation
  generally appears through a non-attorney employee/officer.
- Pierce County District Court sends small claims to **mediation first** —
  expect a mediation session before any trial date; bring the full paper trail.
- Venue/service notes:
  - **Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.** operates Store #4720 in Pierce County — venue
    is clean. Registered agent lookup: [WA SOS Corporations Search](https://ccfs.sos.wa.gov/)
    (Home Depot's WA registered agent is a commercial agent service; confirm
    current agent before filing, as planned in the case file).
  - **Masterbuilt/Premier Specialty Brands LLC d/b/a Middleby Outdoor** is a
    Georgia entity. Confirm it is registered with the WA SOS (it sells at
    retail statewide, so it likely is); if registered, serve its WA registered
    agent. If not registered, service on an out-of-state entity from small
    claims is procedurally harder — ask the Pierce County clerk (253-798-7487)
    about service by mail per RCW 12.40.070 before filing, or consider naming
    Home Depot alone (privity is stronger there anyway, § 3).
- Damages within the cap: $989.39 actual + filing fee + service costs;
  CPA trebling if the judge exercises discretion.

## 9. GAPS / RISKS TO MANAGE (candid assessment)

1. **Privity vs. Masterbuilt** (§ 3) — reframe the Masterbuilt counts as
   express warranty + Magnuson-Moss + CPA, not implied warranty.
2. **Get the actual 1050 warranty text** — the § 2308(b) prominence analysis
   and the § 2-719(3) argument both depend on its exact language.
3. **Handwritten release modification (Option 3)** — legally, adding material
   terms to a signed release operates as a **counteroffer**; Masterbuilt could
   treat it as a rejection and walk. That may be acceptable given Option 2 is
   the planned fallback, but don't expect the modified release to bind them if
   they ship the 1150 without countersigning — a court could find no meeting of
   the minds on the modification. Keep proof you transmitted the modified
   version *before* any performance.
4. **Trebling is discretionary** — plead it, but anchor settlement math on
   $989.39 + costs.
5. **Easter dinner / consequential damages** — emotional impact is compelling
   narrative but not recoverable in dollars here; keep it as context, not a
   line item.
6. **SOL clock:** UCC warranty claims expire ~**July 4, 2027** (RCW 62A.2-725);
   CPA claims four years from the unfair act. No urgency crisis, but don't let
   negotiation drift consume another year.
