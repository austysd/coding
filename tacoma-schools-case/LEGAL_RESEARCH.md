## PART I — THE THREE THINGS THAT CHANGE YOUR STRATEGY

### 1. ⚠️ A parent cannot litigate a child's claims pro se

This is the most important finding in this memo. The strongest claims in this
case — discrimination and retaliation harming **Laila** — belong to *Laila*,
not to you. In both federal court (9th Cir., *Johns v. County of San Diego*,
114 F.3d 874 (1997)) and Washington practice, a non-attorney parent **may not
appear pro se on behalf of a minor child** — representing another person's
legal interests is the practice of law. You can represent *yourself* on claims
that are personally yours (e.g., your PRA claim), but a court will not let you
prosecute Laila's WLAD claims without a lawyer.

Additionally, any settlement of a minor's claim in Washington requires
**court approval** under [SPR 98.16W](https://www.courts.wa.gov/court_rules/pdf/SPR/SUP_SPR_98_16W_00.pdf)
(Pierce County version: [PCLSPR 98.16W](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/90908/PCLSPR-9816W----Settlement-of-Claims-of-Minors-and-Incapacitated-Persons)),
usually with a court-appointed **Settlement Guardian ad Litem** (an independent
attorney) unless the child has independent counsel. Even a pre-suit settlement
check made out for Laila's injuries should go through this process to be safe.

**What this means practically — and it's good news:** WLAD has one-way
attorney-fee shifting (RCW 49.60.030(2)): if Laila wins, the district pays her
attorney's fees *on top of* damages. That makes this case financeable on
**contingency** — no upfront money. Cedar Law quoted you hourly/flat-fee
rates; go back and ask specifically about a **contingency or hybrid
(contingency + statutory fees) arrangement**, and say the words "Floeting
strict liability" and "W.H. v. Olympia" (below). Also continue the planned
outreach (ACLU-WA, NAACP Tacoma, NWJP, WSBA referral). Your pro se plan is not
wasted — everything you assemble makes the case cheaper for a lawyer to take
and stronger to settle.

### 2. ✅ Washington law is dramatically better for you than the standard OCR used

OCR dismissed under the federal Title VI "severe, persistent, or pervasive"
harassment standard. **That is not the Washington standard.** Under the WLAD's
public accommodations provision:

- ***Floeting v. Group Health Cooperative*, 193 Wn.2d 312 (2019)**: an entity
  is **strictly liable** for its employees' discriminatory conduct toward
  customers/patrons in a place of public accommodation. No
  "severe-or-pervasive" showing, no notice-and-failure-to-act showing required
  ([DWT analysis](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2019/02/wa-supreme-court-public-accommodation-employers-ar)).
- ***W.H. v. Olympia School District*, 195 Wn.2d 779 (2020)** (9–0): school
  districts are subject to WLAD public accommodation liability and **may be
  strictly liable for their employees' discrimination**
  ([opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/2020/97630-9.html);
  [Soha & Lang summary](https://www.sohalang.com/washington-supreme-court-holds-school-districts-may-be-strictly-liable-for-employees-discrimination-under-the-wlad/)).

So OCR's "not severe or pervasive enough" dismissal is largely **irrelevant to
the state claim**. The WLAD question is whether Mueller's conduct constituted
discrimination in a place of public accommodation — and if so, the district is
liable for it as a matter of law, regardless of what administrators knew.

### 3. ✅ The CROWN Act puts the hair comment squarely inside the statute

In 2020 Washington amended the WLAD's definition of race
([RCW 49.60.040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=49.60.040), via
[HB 2602](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2019-20/Pdf/Bills/House%20Bills/2602.pdf)):

> "Race" is inclusive of traits historically associated or perceived to be
> associated with race including, but not limited to, **hair texture and
> protective hairstyles** … "protective hairstyles" includes, but is not
> limited to, such hairstyles as **afros, braids, locks, and twists**.

It applies to all WLAD contexts including public accommodation
([MRSC summary](https://mrsc.org/stay-informed/mrsc-insight/june-2020/new-legislation-expands-wlad)).
The Legislature has *specifically declared* that commentary and differential
treatment aimed at Black hairstyles is race discrimination in Washington. A
teacher telling a 6-year-old biracial Black girl "that's not your real hair /
it's not that long" about a fresh protective style is exactly the conduct the
amendment targets. Frame the complaint and demand letter around the CROWN Act
explicitly.

**Also file strategy:** the U.S. Supreme Court in *Cummings v. Premier Rehab
Keller* (2022) held **emotional distress damages are not recoverable under
Title VI** and similar Spending Clause statutes. Since the damages here are
mostly emotional/dignitary plus academic harm, the federal Title VI count is
close to worthless for money damages. **File in Pierce County Superior Court
under the WLAD** — better liability standard (strict), better damages law,
fee-shifting, no federal pleading traps. Keep Title VI out or plead it only as
a secondary theory.

## PART II — PROCEDURAL TRAPS TO CLEAR BEFORE FILING

### A. Government tort claim form — RCW 4.96.020 (60-day wait)

Before suing a Washington school district "for damages arising out of tortious
conduct," a standard tort claim form must be presented to the district's
designated claims agent, and **60 calendar days must elapse** before filing
suit ([RCW 4.96.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=4.96.020)).
The limitations period is tolled during the 60 days. School districts are
covered entities ([Stritmatter guide](https://www.stritmatter.com/washington-government-injury-claims-notice-requirements-deadlines-and-common-mistakes/)).

Whether WLAD claims are "tortious conduct" requiring the form is not cleanly
settled in the published sources I could reach — **so file the form anyway.**
It is free, it takes an afternoon, filing it costs you nothing strategically
(it doubles as a demand-letter predicate), and *not* filing it hands the
district a motion to dismiss the negligence claims and an argument against the
WLAD claims. Your Section 8 plan (demand letter → complaint) is missing this
step entirely — insert it as **Step 6a, before any lawsuit**. Get the form
from the district or Pierce County risk management; identify Laila's claims as
presented by you and Jazmyne as parents/guardians.

### B. Statutes of limitation — you have more time than your case file says

- WLAD: 3 years. But under **RCW 4.16.190, limitations are tolled for
  minors** — Laila's own claims won't expire until three years after her 18th
  birthday (~2040). Real deadlines that are yours: **PRA suit May 12, 2027**;
  any claims belonging to you/Jazmyne personally, ~3 years from the acts
  (early 2029, as your file has it). So the PRA deadline is the only clock
  that actually matters this year — but momentum and witness memory argue for
  moving anyway.

### C. WSHRC complaint (your planned Step 3) — do it, with eyes open

Filing at [hum.wa.gov](https://www.hum.wa.gov/) is free and creates another
official record; the WSHRC handles public accommodation race claims. Caveat:
like OCR, WSHRC can close cases with "no reasonable cause" findings that the
district will then wave around. You do **not** need a WSHRC filing or finding
to sue under WLAD — the private right of action is direct. It's worth filing
mainly for the paper trail and possible free investigation.

### D. Naming Mueller individually

The district is the deep pocket and is strictly liable under *W.H.* Individual
liability for the teacher (e.g., aiding-abetting under RCW 49.60.220) adds
service complexity, motion practice, and sympathy risk. Most plaintiff
attorneys would name the district only, or the district plus Mueller and drop
her at settlement. Decide with counsel; don't let it complicate a pro se tort
claim form.

## PART IV — HONEST MERITS ASSESSMENT

### Strengths (in order)
1. **Strict liability framework** (*Floeting*/*W.H.*) + **CROWN Act** — the
   legal architecture is genuinely favorable and post-2020 modern.
2. **The November 18 email** — contemporaneous, timestamped, BCC-preserved,
   and it *predicts the retaliation pattern in advance*. This is unusually
   good protected-activity evidence.
3. **The report card contradiction** — Mueller's own December 10 document
   ("very well… a joy… good growth," straight 3s) vs. her January 21
   withdrawal-day claims (can't focus, doesn't know sight words, won't meet
   benchmarks). Either she misrepresented the child's progress all year or
   invented concerns at withdrawal; both help you.
4. **District's process failure** — no Civil Rights Coordinator referral, no
   investigation, no written determination despite Policy 5270/5270R. Under
   strict liability you don't *need* this, but it devastates the district's
   good-faith narrative and supports negligence and (weak but pleadable)
   deliberate-indifference theories.
5. **Pattern evidence** — the January 9 parent complaint (dismissiveness
   toward a disabled child's family) plus 11+ absences in five months.

### Weaknesses — prepare for these, because the district will lead with them
1. **The comment is disputed and the witness is six.** Mueller denies it.
   There is no adult witness or recording. A fact-finder must credit a
   6-year-old's report relayed through her parents. Mitigation: Laila told
   her parents immediately (excited-utterance-adjacent consistency), the
   November 18 email locked the account in writing within four days, and the
   CROWN Act framing makes even a "denied but plausible" comment legally
   significant. Answer case-file Question 1 (exact words) carefully and never
   embellish — consistency is worth more than vividness.
2. **Retaliation adverse-action element.** OCR already concluded that raising
   academic concerns at disenrollment isn't "materially adverse." Your better
   framing under WLAD: the adverse action is the **year-long withholding of
   academic information** after the complaint (denying Laila intervention),
   not the January phone call itself. The i-Ready scores ("two grade levels
   below" in reading) printed on the same report card Mueller annotated with
   "doing very well" is the anchor document.
3. **Causation/damages on the academic harm.** The defense will argue:
   (a) i-Ready results were on the report card you received — the family
   *did* have notice; (b) many first-graders test below benchmark and catch
   up; (c) the mid-year transfer itself, not Mueller, caused the setback.
   Countermeasures: Exhibit J (new school's intake assessments) and a
   qualified educator/tutor's written statement quantifying the remediation
   gap are essential before any demand letter goes out.
4. **"No records" cuts both ways on the PRA claim.** If the district truly
   never opened an investigation, then no investigation records exist and
   their non-production is *not* a PRA violation — it's merits evidence of
   the Policy 5270 failure instead. A PRA penalty claim needs proof that
   responsive records **exist and were withheld** (e.g., administrator emails
   about Mueller that must exist given the Jan 9 complaint went to the
   principal). Penalties run $0–$100/day per record group at the court's
   discretion (*Yousoufian* factors), plus **mandatory** attorney fees/costs
   for a prevailing requester — and note the PRA claim is *yours*, so you
   genuinely can run it pro se.

**Non-economic damages (emotional distress/dignitary harm to Laila):** WLAD
allows full compensatory damages. Comparables for a single discriminatory
incident against a student with no physical injury cluster in the low-to-mid
five figures; the retaliation/withholding year and the age of the child push

**Scenario ranges (total settlement value):**
| Posture | Realistic range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pro se, pre-suit demand | **$10,000–$40,000** + non-monetary terms | District knows a pro se parent cannot try the child's claims or recover fees; discounts accordingly |
| Represented (contingency), tort claim filed, complaint drafted | **$60,000–$175,000** | Ray comp + fee-shifting exposure + strict liability does the work |

| PRA claim (yours, separable) | **$0–$25,000** + mandatory fees if you prove withholding | Entirely dependent on proving responsive records exist |

**Non-monetary terms worth real value — put them in every demand:** district-
funded tutoring/compensatory education for Laila; written apology; expungement/
correction of any negative records; Policy 5270 compliance audit; CROWN
Act-specific staff training at Blix. (The Ray settlement included a written
apology — precedent that Tacoma will give one.)

**Demand-letter anchor:** with counsel, an opening demand of **$150,000–
$250,000** citing *Floeting*, *W.H.*, the CROWN Act, and the Ray settlement is
defensible. Pro se, an opening demand around **$75,000** with the same
citations, settling toward the pro se range above, is realistic. Do not open
at your floor.
